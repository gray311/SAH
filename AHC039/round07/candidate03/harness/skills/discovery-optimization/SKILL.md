# Sardine-Exclusion-Aware Polygon Optimization for Fish Capture\n\n## Problem Recap\n- Maximize: mackerels_inside - sardines_inside + 1\n- Polygon: axis-aligned (edges parallel to x or y), max 1000 vertices, perimeter at most 400,000\n- Fish: N=5000 mackerels (type=1), N=5000 sardines (type=-1) at distinct integer coordinates 0 to 10^5\n- Time limit: approximately 2.0 seconds for internal search, use full budget actively\n\n## Key Insight: Sardines are the Barrier\nEvery sardine inside the polygon reduces your score by 1. The optimal polygon captures dense mackerel regions while EXCLUDING sardine clusters. Focus on:\n1. Which sardines are "excludable" (near mackerel cluster boundaries)\n2. Which mackerel clusters are isolated from sardines\n3. Shapes (L, stepped, multi-lobed) that cut through sardine groups\n\n## Core Strategy: Probe-Guided Sardine-Aware Search\n\n### Phase 1: Fast Fish Counting with Grid/KD-Tree\n- Pre-process fish into spatial index (grid with 500 to 1000 cell size, or KD-tree)\n- Enables O(1) or O(log N) fish count for any axis-aligned rectangle\n- Critical for testing many polygon variants quickly\n\n### Phase 2: Sardine Exclusion Analysis\nBefore constructing polygons, identify:\n- Boundary sardines: Sardines within 200 to 500 units of mackerel bounding box edges\n- Deep sardines: Sardines surrounded by mackerels (hardest to exclude)\n- Sardine clusters: Groups of 3 or more sardines close together (one shape can exclude all)\n\nAlgorithm:\n1. Compute mackerel bounding box [min_x, max_x] times [min_y, max_y]\n2. Flag sardines where s.x < max_mx + 300 or s.x > min_mx - 300 or similar\n3. These are "easy exclude" candidates\n\n### Phase 3: Smart Polygon Construction Pipeline\n\n#### Shape Types to Try:\n\nA. Bounding Box Variants (baseline)\n- Simple bbox of all mackerels\n- Shrink bbox to exclude boundary sardines\n- Expand bbox if it captures more mackerels without hitting many new sardines\n\nB. L-Shaped Polygons (exclude one corner sardine cluster)\nCapture a dense mackerel corner while cutting off a sardine-heavy region:\n\nC. Stepped/Staircase Polygons (follow mackerel density contours)\nCreate staircase that follows high-density mackerel regions, steps avoid sardine clusters between clusters\n\nD. Multi-Rect Union (capture separate mackerel clusters, exclude sardines in between)\nCombine multiple rectangles with gaps where sardines live, must ensure non-self-intersecting output\n\n### Phase 4: Iterative Refinement with Probe Guidance\n\n1. Generate candidate shapes: bbox, L-shapes, stepped, multi-rect (3 to 5 per shape type)\n2. Probe all candidates: Use probe_solution to get approximate scores in approximately 10 seconds each\n3. Rank by probe score: Keep top 3\n4. Full evaluate top 3: Get exact scores, pick best\n5. Local refinement: From best polygon, try edge perturbations (plus/minus 1 to 20 units)\n   Each perturbation: check validity, count fish (use spatial index), keep if improves\n6. Repeat: Continue refinement loop until 1.5 seconds elapsed or no improvement for 0.2 seconds\n\n### Phase 5: Time Budget Management (critical!)\n- Total time: 2.0 seconds, use approximately 1.8 seconds for search (0.1 second safety margin)\n- Breakdown:\n  - 0.1 seconds: Setup (read input, build spatial index)\n  - 0.3 seconds: Initial bbox plus 3 variants (probe)\n  - 0.5 seconds: Full evaluate top 2, pick best\n  - 1.0 seconds: Iterative refinement loop (edge perturbations)\n  - 0.1 seconds: Final output generation\n- Early termination: If score stops improving for 0.15 seconds, finalize current best\n- Always output VALID polygon on exit\n\n## Common Pitfalls\n- Do not hardcode a single polygon - implement ACTIVE search loop\n- Do not ignore sardine exclusion - they can make or break your score\n- Do not use full evaluation for every variant - use probe_solution for ranking\n- Do not forget perimeter constraint - check after each polygon construction\n- Do not over-complexify - simple bbox plus smart refinements often beat complex shapes
