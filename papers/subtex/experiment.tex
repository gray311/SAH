\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

\paragraph{Evaluation Tasks.}  Eleven discovery problems in three families,
drawn from the evaluation suite of the discovery-finetuned Finch
models~\citep{finch} so that a matched-scale finetuned baseline exists for
every task: \emph{mathematical construction} (\textbf{Erd\H{o}s},
\textbf{AC1}, \textbf{AC2}, \textbf{CP}, \textbf{Hadamard}), popularized by
AlphaEvolve and its
replications~\citep{alphaevolve,alphaevolvemath,openevolve};
\emph{heuristic-contest programming} (\textbf{ahc039}, \textbf{ahc058}),
scored under the official AtCoder 150-case
protocol~\citep{ahc,alebench}; and \emph{systems algorithm design}
(\textbf{EPLB}, \textbf{PRISM}, \textbf{LLM-SQL}, \textbf{Txn}) from the
ADRS suite~\citep{barbarians}.  Every task ships a task description, a seed
program with an editable region, a black-box evaluator, and a hard
per-rollout budget of evaluator calls; scores are reported in each task's
native units, and the executor never sees the evaluator's implementation or
ground truth (\S\ref{sec:method-safety}).

\paragraph{Baselines.}  \emph{Finch-9B}~\citep{finch}, a 9B model
discovery-finetuned on evolution-trajectory corpora, is the matched-scale
external bar: beating it is the evidence that a learned harness substitutes
for discovery finetuning.  \emph{Published $\le$10B SOTA} aggregates the
best previously reported score per task across systems at or below 10B
parameters (per-task sources in Appendix~\ref{app:full-results}); because
it pools many specialized systems against one frozen executor, we read it as
calibration.  Our own system appears under three conditions that share the
executor, evaluator, seed program, initial harness, and per-rollout budget,
and differ only in what the reward updates: \emph{\method~(initial)}, a
fixed hand-written harness---no adaptation; \emph{\method~(context)},
proposer weights frozen, adaptation only through what the proposer
reads---the incumbent, the experience digest, and a leak-guarded analyst
brief distilled by the same frozen executor; and \emph{\method~(weight)},
the trained proposer---our full method.

\paragraph{Implementation Details.}  The executor is
Qwen3.5-9B~\citep{qwen3}, served frozen with vLLM~\citep{vllm}; no gradient
or test-time update ever touches it.  For the outer search, we sample
$K{=}8$ harness specs per task per round at 20--25 evaluator calls per
rollout; cascade rounds screen $K{=}16$ candidates at 5 calls each and
promote the top four to full budget.  For proposer training, we attach a
LoRA adapter~\citep{lora} (rank 64, $\alpha{=}128$; 116.4M trainable
parameters) to the same base model and run offline-replay RLOO
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:advantage}) for 3 epochs per step at global batch size 8,
with the adapter persisting across steps and a KL anchor to the frozen
base.  Winning programs are re-evaluated five times for stability.  Full
hyperparameters, per-round costs, and the aarch64 evaluation protocol are
presented in Appendix~\ref{app:infra}.

\paragraph{Learned harnesses carry a frozen executor to the $\le$10B state
of the art.}  Figure~\ref{fig:radar} and Table~\ref{tab:conditions}
summarize the main comparison; the complete per-task table is in
Appendix~\ref{app:full-results}.  \method{} beats the discovery-finetuned
Finch-9B on all eleven tasks, by margins from a fraction of a percent
(Erd\H{o}s, EPLB) to $+36\%$ (ahc058), and sets the outright best published
$\le$10B score on five: the Erd\H{o}s constant ($0.380919$ vs.\ $0.380932$),
ahc039, ahc058, EPLB, and PRISM ($26.26$ vs.\ $24.70$).  A sixth entry,
LLM-SQL, exceeds the prior best but traces to a curated note rather than to
executor discovery, and we exclude it from every claim; its provenance is
disclosed in Appendix~\ref{app:full-results}.  On Erd\H{o}s the
frozen executor also edges past the best known human construction
($0.380927$): the winning program is a step-function optimizer the executor
wrote itself---an $800$-interval discretization whose overlap integral is
evaluated by FFT correlation and minimized by $120$k gradient steps over
three restarts.  The remaining gaps are stated as plainly: circle packing
stops at $95\%$ of the ThetaEvolve record, Hadamard lands within $1\%$ of a
record that used large-model meta-information, and AC1, AC2, and the
transaction column stay with prior systems.

\paragraph{Where adaptation happens matters more than that it happens.}
Table~\ref{tab:conditions} isolates the three conditions.  The fixed
hand-written harness beats Finch-9B on just two tasks and collapses on
LLM-SQL ($0.0934$), so scaffolding alone does not explain the result.
Letting the frozen proposer adapt only its \emph{context} already recovers
most of the distance: it improves on the fixed harness on ten of eleven
tasks (PRISM is the exception) and on EPLB is the strongest system in the
table ($0.1278$).  Training the proposer's \emph{weights} adds a further
gain on eight tasks and ties the context arm on two more, making
internalized experience the stronger default; the single reversal (EPLB)
and the two ties keep this an ordering rather than a uniform dominance, and
the arms are not budget-matched.

Table~\ref{tab:closed} compares \method{} against OpenEvolve driving

\vspace{2mm}
\begin{takeawaybox}{Updating the Proposer is a Traceable Route to Self-Improvement}
Routing the reward into the proposer---rather than into the executor's
weights---makes every improvement an inspectable artifact: a prompt, a
skill, a generated tool, a control parameter.  This traceability costs no
strength: the frozen 9B executor beats the discovery-finetuned Finch-9B on
all eleven tasks and sets the published $\le$10B state of the art on five,
records previously held by systems built on finetuned executors.
\end{takeawaybox}

\subsection{Reward Routing: Why Update the Proposer?}
\label{sec:exp-routing}

A reward earned by a test-time rollout can be spent in three places: the
\emph{executor's weights}, as in test-time RL for
discovery~\citep{tttdiscover,thetaevolve}; the \emph{proposer's context},
by distilling past rollouts into an analyst brief the proposer re-reads
each round; or the \emph{proposer's weights}---our choice.  The routes are
not equally available: executor updates are closed whenever the model is
frozen by policy or served behind an API, and they are not attributable to
any nameable mechanism after the fact.  We compare the three under a shared
protocol---same frozen executor, same evaluator, same initial harness and
seed program---so that the routes differ only in what the reward updates.

Two facts anchor the comparison before any curve is drawn.  First, the
routes differ sharply in trajectory budget: published test-time RL for
discovery trains on $512$ rollouts per step for $50$ steps---$25{,}600$
executor trajectories per problem~\citep{tttdiscover}---whereas the entire
\method{} campaign spends fewer than two thousand executor trajectories per
task and only 27 proposer updates, since every trajectory doubles as a
group-relative training signal.  Second, the routes differ in what remains
afterwards: an executor update leaves new weights; a context update leaves
a brief the proposer must keep re-reading; a proposer update leaves a
policy that writes better harnesses---and the harness itself stays an
inspectable artifact either way.  Figure~\ref{fig:reward_route} plots the
three routes on a common trajectory axis.

\vspace{2mm}
\begin{takeawaybox}{Proposer Updates Buy the Most per Trajectory}
Routing reward into the proposer is the only option that is always
available (the executor may be frozen or behind an API), keeps every
improvement attributable to an inspectable harness, and reaches its
records on orders of magnitude fewer executor trajectories than published
test-time RL for discovery.
\end{takeawaybox}
