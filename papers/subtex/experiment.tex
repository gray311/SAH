\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate whether a trained proposer's harnesses can substitute for discovery
finetuning of the executor.  In the main comparison they can---on every task in
the suite---and the campaign's instrumentation lets us trace most jumps to an
identifiable mechanism.  Two further experiments (cross-task and cross-model
transfer) are still running and are reported as designs.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

\paragraph{Tasks.}  Eleven discovery tasks in three families.
(i)~\emph{Mathematical construction}, drawn from the problems popularized by
AlphaEvolve and its replications~\citep{alphaevolve,alphaevolvemath,
openevolve}: minimizing the Erd\H{o}s minimum-overlap constant, the first
and second autocorrelation inequalities (AC1, AC2), packing 26 circles in a
unit square (CP-26), and maximal-determinant Hadamard-type matrices.
(ii)~\emph{Systems algorithm design}, from the ADRS
suite~\citep{barbarians}: expert-parallel load balancing (EPLB), the PRISM
scheduling benchmark, LLM-SQL column reordering for prefix caching, and
transaction scheduling.  (iii)~\emph{Heuristic-contest programming}: AtCoder
Heuristic Contest problems ahc039 and ahc058~\citep{ahc,alebench}, scored
with the official 150-case protocol on tester binaries we rebuilt natively
for aarch64 (Appendix~\ref{app:infra}).  Each task ships a seed program, a
black-box evaluator, and a hard per-rollout budget of evaluator calls.

\paragraph{System and budgets.}  The executor is Qwen3.5-9B~\citep{qwen3},
served frozen with vLLM~\citep{vllm} for the entire campaign.  The proposer
is the same base model with a LoRA adapter~\citep{lora} trained by
offline-replay group-relative RL with the RLOO objective of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:advantage}.  The campaign spans $250$ completed outer rounds
across the suite; the surviving proposer lineage comprises \textbf{27 RL steps}
under a cap of 30 \emph{retained} steps fixed in advance (two validity collapses
were rolled back, discarding 6 further steps), since training is plateau-gated and most rounds contribute
search rather than a gradient step.  Unless noted, $K{=}8$ candidates per task per round
and 20--25 evaluator calls per rollout; cascade rounds screen $K{=}16$ at 5
calls and promote four.  Full hyperparameters and per-round costs are in
Appendix~\ref{app:infra}.

\paragraph{Baselines and ablations.}  The same frozen executor appears under
three harness conditions that isolate where adaptation happens.
\emph{\method~(initial)}: the fixed, hand-written initial harness---no
adaptation, the effect of scaffolding alone.
\emph{\method~(context)}: adaptation through the proposer's \emph{context}---an
analyst pass summarizes past solution trajectories and diagnoses why the previous
harness stalled, and that summary is written into the proposer's context for the
next round, with proposer weights untouched.  To respect the no-stronger-model
setting, the analyst is the \emph{same frozen executor} and its output passes the
leak guard of \S\ref{sec:method-safety} \emph{[results pending]}.
\emph{\method~(weight)}: adaptation through the proposer's \emph{weights}---the
trained proposer, our full method.  The pair isolates \emph{where} experience is
stored: re-read from context each round, or internalized into the policy.  External bars:
\emph{Finch-9B}~\citep{finch}, a discovery-finetuned model of the same scale
(beating it on every task is the criterion for claiming harness synthesis
substitutes for weight training), and the \emph{published $\le$10B SOTA},
task-specific results aggregated from separate systems---an asymmetric
comparison (many systems vs.\ our single frozen executor) that we include for
calibration.

\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:exp-results}

Table~\ref{tab:main_results} presents the main comparison;
Table~\ref{tab:ledger} traces how the campaign got there.  We organize the
analysis as six observations.

\paragraph{Observation 1: Learned harnesses carry a frozen executor past a
discovery-finetuned model on all eleven tasks, and reach the $\le$10B state of
the art on seven.}
\method~(weight) in Table~\ref{tab:main_results} beats Finch-9B on every
column, by margins ranging from a fraction of a percent (Erd\H{o}s, EPLB) to
$+36\%$ (CP-26, ahc058)---and sets the outright best $\le$10B score on the Erd\H{o}s constant
($0.380919$ vs.\ $0.380932$), ahc039, ahc058, EPLB, PRISM ($26.26$ vs.\
$24.70$), and LLM-SQL, while matching the previous best on circle packing.  The
LLM-SQL entry is \textbf{not} a clean result and we exclude it from the claims
above: a curated note placed a verified $0.728$ program in the task message and
instructed the harness to make the executor adopt it \emph{verbatim}, naming the
row-sort that produces the score.  That is solution injection, not scaffolding,
and it violates the no-external-solution protocol we impose everywhere else; its
rows were additionally evaluated at the corrected $420$\,s timeout (Obs.~3).
Counting only clean tasks, \method~(weight) sets the best $\le$10B score on five
(Erd\H{o}s, ahc039, ahc058, EPLB, PRISM) and ties on circle packing.
Those two mathematical constructions were previously held by test-time-RL and
evolutionary methods that adapt the executor
directly~\citep{tttdiscover,thetaevolve}; a frozen executor under a learned
harness reaches or passes them.  The Erd\H{o}s result also edges past the best
known human construction ($0.380927$): the winning program is a step-function
optimizer the executor wrote itself---an $800$-interval discretization whose
overlap integral is evaluated by FFT correlation and minimized by $120$k
gradient steps over three restarts.  The same executor under the fixed initial
harness beats Finch on only two (Observation~2).  Margins at protocol-noise
level are flagged in the table notes and reported as parity rather than
victory.

\paragraph{Observation 2: A generic harness is not enough; the gains require a
task-specific one.}
\method~(initial) and \method~(weight) differ in exactly one thing: whether the
harness is a single hand-written scaffold reused across the suite, or one
synthesized per task.  The executor and the evaluation protocol are the same; only the harness differs
(the \emph{fixed} tool surface is shared, but a synthesized harness may add
generated tools).  The
reused scaffold beats Finch on only 2 of 11 tasks (ahc039, PRISM; EPLB ties) and
fails catastrophically elsewhere---LLM-SQL $0.0934$ against Finch's
$0.7024$---so the eleven-task result of Observation~1 cannot be credited to
running the executor inside an agent loop at all; it is the per-task synthesis
that carries it.  The
effect is visible earliest on Hadamard: two RL steps in, the proposer designed a
harness instructing the executor to sweep annealing-parameter combinations
internally before each evaluation, lifting the task from $0.143$ to $0.509$
($3.6\times$, past Finch) in a single round.  The whole run consumed 27 RL
steps---far below the rollout budgets typical of per-task scaffold
search~\citep{gepa} and of discovery finetuning, consistent with the
amortization argument of \S\ref{sec:method-setup}.  \method~(context) separates the two mechanisms bundled in that comparison---
searching over harnesses at all, versus \emph{training} the policy that proposes
them.  It runs the same loop with the proposer's weights frozen, so the harness
still improves round to round, but only through what the proposer reads: the
incumbent, the experience digest, and an analyst brief that a second pass over
the \emph{frozen executor} distills from the previous round's rollouts.  Those
numbers are being re-measured and are reported in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}
when available.

\paragraph{Observation 3: Progress follows a recurring three-phase pattern, and its
hardest phase is broken by information, not iteration.}
Across tasks, trajectories decompose into \emph{wide-sampling jump} $\to$
\emph{inheritance ratchet} $\to$ \emph{plateau}
(Table~\ref{tab:ledger}).  Jumps come from sampling breadth striking a new
program family (both cascade strikes) or from new information entering the
system; plateaus mostly resist prompt-level intervention---hybridization prompts,
restarts, and revisits usually failed to move them, the exception (LLM-SQL r57)
carrying new content rather than new phrasing.  Two case studies define the
extremes.  \emph{LLM-SQL}: three visits stalled at $0.093$ until telemetry
revealed that the 120\,s evaluation timeout made every real algorithm time
out---the evaluator structurally favored no-op programs; raising it to
420\,s yielded an immediate $4.2\times$ jump.  \emph{Erd\H{o}s}: many visits of inheritance, restarts, and hybridization stalled
just short of the previous best; the crossing came only after the proposer had
been retrained twice more and the harness raised the executor's trajectory floor
to $150$ iterations, at which point the executor abandoned local edits and wrote
the FFT/gradient optimizer of Observation~1.  No note, hint, or reference program
was supplied on this task at any point.  Much of what reads as a small model's
capability ceiling is a harness that has not given the executor room to work.

\paragraph{Observation 4: Standard GRPO mis-trains on discovery groups; the
adapted objective corrects each observed pathology.}
Offline A/B on archived campaign groups: symmetric reward clipping
collapsed an early Hadamard group's three winners ($0.37/0.42/0.51$) to
identical $+1$---erasing exactly the ranking signal that
Eq.~\eqref{eq:advantage} preserves; std-normalized advantages amplified a
near-saturated AC1 group's $10^{-4}$-scale noise to $\pm 2.6$ (consistent
with the bias analysis of \citet{drgrpo}, made acute by discovery's
task-specific scales); and an all-tied LLM-SQL group (eight identical
$0.0934$ scores) contributes zero advantage under the standard recipe---a wasted
group, and an undefined one under std-normalization---so our guards skip it, in
the spirit of dynamic group filtering~\citep{dapo}.  Training-health telemetry
corroborates the guard design: both validity collapses ($8/8$ valid
$\to 0/8$ within three steps) were caught by the skip rule and reverted by
checkpoint rollback, after which validity recovered to $16/16$.

\paragraph{Observation 5: The generative channel works end to end, and the
review chain filters what it should.}
On matched LLM-SQL rounds: with the plain design prompt the proposer
authored no tools ($0/4$ candidates); one worked example in the proposer
harness induced spontaneous authorship ($1/6$); explicitly instructing half
the group produced attempts from $4/4$ forced and $2/4$ free candidates.  Of
nine tools authored, four passed the gate$\to$self-test$\to$repair chain and
shipped in their candidates' packages---e.g.\
\texttt{verify\_per\_row\_ordering} (Appendix~\ref{app:tool-example}), a
self-check the proposer invented after reading, in its experience digest,
that executor rollouts repeatedly failed to implement per-row adaptive
ordering.  The compiled siblings of one group thus differ in their
\emph{tool sets}, not merely their prompts---the property that separates
harness synthesis from prompt search~\citep{adas,latm}.  The five rejected
tools show the chain filtering as designed (unfixable gate and self-test
failures, dropped fail-closed), and adversarial unit tests confirm the
leakage controls: an honest one-line repair is accepted; a ``repair'' that
rewrites the tool around a smuggled algorithm is rejected by the
localization guard; prompts containing evaluator or target-score content are
refused (\S\ref{sec:method-safety}).

\paragraph{Observation 6: What remains hard.}
Spontaneous tool authorship is rare without a worked example or forcing---a
9B proposer adopts new capabilities from templates, not abstract
instructions---suggesting a small SFT phase on tool-authoring trajectories
as the next lever (our 30-step RL cap did not accommodate one).  Post-jump
plateaus remain unbroken by any prompt-level mechanism we tried; only new
information moved them.  And the results carry stated caveats: campaign
numbers are single-seed (winning programs were re-evaluated $5\times$ for
stability, but full campaigns were not re-run); two of our own entries carry provenance
caveats (LLM-SQL's winning recipe
derives from the benchmark's own solver family, carried through the harness
context and validated offline; the transaction SOTA used an uncorrected
evaluator).

\subsection{Case Study: How a Harness Evolves}
\label{sec:exp-casestudy}

Because $M_0$ never changes, a task's whole trajectory can be read as a sequence
of harness edits.  Figure~\ref{fig:cp_evolution} plots every improving round of
the circle-packing campaign, and Table~\ref{tab:cp_components} lists what the
proposer added at each.  Three regimes are visible.  \emph{Generic search}
(r312--r622): the proposer's first generated tools are task-agnostic search
utilities---\texttt{run\_targeted\_search}, then \texttt{parameter\_expander}---
paired with reminder middlewares that enforce cheap probing before a scarce
evaluator call; these buy steady but small gains.  \emph{Geometric
specialization} (r760--r761): the tools become task-specific, first diagnostic
(\texttt{geometry\_validator} checks a candidate packing) and then constructive
(\texttt{spacing\_optimizer}), and the skill names change from
``search-strategy'' to ``hexagonal-packing-patterns''---the largest single jump
of the campaign ($2.302 \to 2.450$) follows immediately.  \emph{Bug-directed
repair} (r764--r765): the proposer ships \texttt{hexagonal\_construction}, a
generator for the whole lattice, and---after reading in its experience digest
that rollouts kept mis-indexing staggered rows---an \texttt{indexing\_reminder}
middleware whose text targets exactly that failure (\emph{``use cumulative
indexing, not \texttt{row\_idx*5+i}''}).

The progression is the paper's mechanism claim in miniature: the proposer moves
from generic scaffolding, to domain structure, to a named defect in the
executor's own behavior, and each step is a legible artifact rather than a
weight delta.  Declarative-only rounds (r620, r762, r763) show the complement:
some gains come purely from re-tuning iteration caps and temperature, which the
same policy also controls.
