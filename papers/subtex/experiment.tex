\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

\paragraph{Tasks.}  We evaluate on eleven discovery problems in three
families, drawn from the evaluation suite of the discovery-finetuned Finch
models~\citep{finch} so that a matched-scale finetuned baseline exists for
every column.  (i)~\emph{Mathematical construction} (five tasks),
popularized by AlphaEvolve and its
replications~\citep{alphaevolve,alphaevolvemath,openevolve}:
\textbf{Erd\H{o}s} minimizes the Erd\H{o}s minimum-overlap constant;
\textbf{AC1} and \textbf{AC2} sharpen the first and second autocorrelation
inequalities; \textbf{CP} packs 26 circles into a unit square, maximizing
the radius sum; \textbf{Hadamard} searches for maximal-determinant
Hadamard-type matrices.  (ii)~\emph{Heuristic-contest programming} (two
tasks; \emph{Algorithm Engineering} in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}):
AtCoder Heuristic Contest problems \textbf{ahc039} and
\textbf{ahc058}~\citep{ahc,alebench}, scored under the official 150-case
protocol on tester binaries we rebuilt natively for aarch64
(Appendix~\ref{app:infra}).  (iii)~\emph{Systems algorithm design} (four
tasks; \emph{System Performance} in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}), from the
ADRS suite~\citep{barbarians}: \textbf{EPLB} balances expert-parallel load
across GPUs, \textbf{PRISM} schedules model placements, \textbf{LLM-SQL}
reorders table columns for prefix caching, and \textbf{Txn} schedules
transactions.  Every task ships a natural-language description, a seed
program with an editable region, a black-box evaluator returning a scalar
score, and a hard per-rollout budget of evaluator calls; the executor never
sees the evaluator's implementation or ground truth
(\S\ref{sec:method-safety}).

\paragraph{System.}  The executor is Qwen3.5-9B~\citep{qwen3}, served frozen
with vLLM~\citep{vllm} for the entire campaign; no gradient, adapter, or
test-time update ever touches it.  The proposer is the same base model with a
LoRA adapter~\citep{lora}, trained by offline-replay group-relative RL with
the RLOO objective of Eq.~\eqref{eq:advantage}.  Unless noted, a round
samples $K{=}8$ harness specs per task and each rollout spends 20--25
evaluator calls; wide (cascade) rounds screen $K{=}16$ candidates at 5 calls
each and promote the top four to full budget (\S\ref{sec:method-search}).

\paragraph{Budgets.}  The main campaign comprises $235$ completed outer
rounds.  The surviving proposer lineage holds \textbf{27 RL steps} under a
cap of 30 retained steps fixed in advance: training is plateau-gated, so most
rounds contribute search rather than a gradient step, and two validity
collapses were rolled back, discarding 6 further steps.  Round identifiers
are global counters shared by every run on the cluster, so they are sparse
and exceed the round count: the main campaign occupies ids up to $969$, and
later single-task pushes carry four-digit ids.  Full hyperparameters and
per-round costs are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:infra}.

\paragraph{Conditions.}  The same frozen executor appears under three
harness conditions that isolate \emph{where} adaptation happens.
\emph{\method~(initial)}: a single fixed, hand-written harness reused across
the suite---scaffolding with no adaptation.
\emph{\method~(context)}: proposer weights stay frozen; adaptation flows
only through what the proposer \emph{reads}---the incumbent, the experience
digest, and an analyst brief that a second pass over the same frozen
executor distills from the previous round's rollouts, filtered by the leak
guard of \S\ref{sec:method-safety}.
\emph{\method~(weight)}: the trained proposer---our full method.  All
three conditions share the same frozen executor, evaluator, seed program,
initial harness, and per-rollout evaluator budget; they differ only in what
the reward is allowed to update.  The context/weight pair thus isolates
where experience is stored: re-read from context every round, or
internalized into the policy.

\paragraph{Baselines and protocol.}  \emph{Finch-9B}~\citep{finch}, a 9B
model discovery-finetuned on evolution-trajectory corpora, is the
matched-scale external bar: beating it on a column is the evidence that a
learned harness substitutes for discovery finetuning there.
\emph{Published $\le$10B SOTA} aggregates the best previously reported score
per task across systems at or below 10B parameters (per-task sources in the
notes of Table~\ref{tab:main_results}); because it pools many specialized
systems against one frozen executor, we read it as calibration rather than
as a head-to-head baseline.  Scores are reported in each task's native
units.  Winning programs were re-evaluated five times for stability, and
validity guards are applied uniformly to our own results: schedule legality
for transaction scheduling, official-tester parity for the AHC tasks, and
the corrected $420$\,s evaluation timeout for LLM-SQL.

\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:exp-results}

Table~\ref{tab:main_results} presents the main comparison;
Table~\ref{tab:ledger} traces how the campaign got there.  We organize the
analysis as six observations.

\paragraph{Observation 1: Learned harnesses carry a frozen executor past a
discovery-finetuned model on all eleven tasks, and set the $\le$10B state of
the art on five.}
\method~(weight) in Table~\ref{tab:main_results} beats Finch-9B on every
column, by margins ranging from a fraction of a percent (Erd\H{o}s, EPLB) to
$+36\%$ (ahc058)---and sets the outright best $\le$10B score on five: the
Erd\H{o}s constant ($0.380919$ vs.\ $0.380932$), ahc039, ahc058, EPLB, and
PRISM ($26.26$ vs.\ $24.70$).  A sixth entry, LLM-SQL, exceeds the prior best
but is \textbf{not} a clean result and we exclude it from every claim: a
curated note placed a verified $0.728$ program in the task message and
instructed the harness to make the executor adopt it \emph{verbatim}, naming
the row-sort that produces the score.  That is solution injection, not
scaffolding, and it violates the no-external-solution protocol we impose
everywhere else; its rows were additionally evaluated at the corrected
$420$\,s timeout (Obs.~3).  On circle packing the campaign closes most of the
gap---$1.478 \to 2.502$ under the learned proposer---but stops at $95\%$ of
the ThetaEvolve record ($2.636$), and we report that as a miss, not a tie.
The Erd\H{o}s record was previously held by test-time-RL and evolutionary
methods that adapt the executor
directly~\citep{tttdiscover,thetaevolve}; a frozen executor under a learned
harness passes it, and also edges past the best
known human construction ($0.380927$): the winning program is a step-function
optimizer the executor wrote itself---an $800$-interval discretization whose
overlap integral is evaluated by FFT correlation and minimized by $120$k
gradient steps over three restarts.  The same executor under the fixed initial
harness beats Finch on only two (Observation~2).  Margins at protocol-noise
level we read as parity rather than victory.

\paragraph{Observation 2: A generic harness is not enough; the gains require a
task-specific one.}
\method~(initial) and \method~(weight) differ in exactly one thing: whether the
harness is a single hand-written scaffold reused across the suite, or one
synthesized per task.  The executor and the evaluation protocol are the same; only the harness differs
(the \emph{fixed} tool surface is shared, but a synthesized harness may add
generated tools).  The
reused scaffold beats Finch on only 2 of 11 tasks (ahc039, PRISM; EPLB ties) and
fails catastrophically elsewhere---LLM-SQL $0.0934$ against Finch's
$0.7024$---so the eleven-task result of Observation~1 cannot be credited to
running the executor inside an agent loop at all; it is the per-task synthesis
that carries it.  The
effect is visible earliest on Hadamard: two RL steps in, the proposer designed a
harness instructing the executor to sweep annealing-parameter combinations
internally before each evaluation, lifting the task from $0.143$ to $0.509$
($3.6\times$, past Finch) in a single round.  The whole run consumed 27 RL
steps---far below the rollout budgets typical of per-task scaffold
search~\citep{gepa} and of discovery finetuning, consistent with the
amortization argument of \S\ref{sec:method-setup}.  \method~(context) separates the two mechanisms bundled in that comparison---
searching over harnesses at all, versus \emph{training} the policy that proposes
them.  It runs the same loop with the proposer's weights frozen, so the harness
still improves round to round, but only through what the proposer reads: the
incumbent, the experience digest, and an analyst brief that a second pass over
the \emph{frozen executor} distills from the previous round's rollouts.  Figure~\ref{fig:score_compute} plots all three
routes on a common compute axis.  Both effects are real, and they are ordered
on most of the suite.  Context adaptation alone improves on the fixed harness
on ten of the eleven tasks (PRISM is the exception)---circle packing
$1.478 \to 2.370$, Hadamard $0.361 \to 0.517$, ahc058
$1.34 \to 5.63 \times 10^{8}$, Erd\H{o}s $0.4566 \to 0.3958$---so a frozen
proposer that merely reads a better context is already far better than a fixed
scaffold.  Training the proposer then adds a further gain on eight of the
eleven, ties the context arm on two (AC1, ahc039), and the strict ordering
\method~(initial) $<$ \method~(context) $<$ \method~(weight) holds on seven.
EPLB is the one column that runs the other way---the context arm ends highest
($0.1278$ vs.\ $0.1272$)---so internalizing experience into weights is the
stronger default, not a uniform dominance.  One caveat keeps this an ordering
rather than an exchange rate: the arms are not budget-matched.

\paragraph{Observation 3: Progress follows a recurring three-phase pattern, and its
hardest phase is broken by information, not iteration.}
Across tasks, trajectories decompose into \emph{wide-sampling jump} $\to$
\emph{inheritance ratchet} $\to$ \emph{plateau}
(Table~\ref{tab:ledger}).  Jumps come from sampling breadth striking a new
program family (both cascade strikes) or from new information entering the
system; plateaus mostly resist prompt-level intervention---hybridization prompts,
restarts, and revisits usually failed to move them, the exception (LLM-SQL r57)
carrying new content rather than new phrasing.  Two case studies define the
extremes.  \emph{LLM-SQL}: three visits stalled at $0.093$ until telemetry
revealed that the 120\,s evaluation timeout made every real algorithm time
out---the evaluator structurally favored no-op programs; raising it to
420\,s yielded an immediate $4.2\times$ jump.  \emph{Erd\H{o}s}: many visits of inheritance, restarts, and hybridization stalled
just short of the previous best; the crossing came only after the proposer had
been retrained twice more and the harness raised the executor's trajectory floor
to $150$ iterations, at which point the executor abandoned local edits and wrote
the FFT/gradient optimizer of Observation~1.  No note, hint, or reference program
was supplied on this task at any point.  Much of what reads as a small model's
capability ceiling is a harness that has not given the executor room to work.

\paragraph{Observation 4: Standard GRPO mis-trains on discovery groups; the
adapted objective corrects each observed pathology.}
Offline A/B on archived campaign groups: symmetric reward clipping
collapsed an early Hadamard group's three winners ($0.37/0.42/0.51$) to
identical $+1$---erasing exactly the ranking signal that
Eq.~\eqref{eq:advantage} preserves; std-normalized advantages amplified a
near-saturated AC1 group's $10^{-4}$-scale noise to $\pm 2.6$ (consistent
with the bias analysis of \citet{drgrpo}, made acute by discovery's
task-specific scales); and an all-tied LLM-SQL group (eight identical
$0.0934$ scores) contributes zero advantage under the standard recipe---a wasted
group, and an undefined one under std-normalization---so our guards skip it, in
the spirit of dynamic group filtering~\citep{dapo}.  Training-health telemetry
corroborates the guard design: both validity collapses ($8/8$ valid
$\to 0/8$ within three steps) were caught by the skip rule and reverted by
checkpoint rollback, after which validity recovered to $16/16$.

\paragraph{Observation 5: The generative channel works end to end, and the
review chain filters what it should.}
On matched LLM-SQL rounds, authorship tracked what the proposer's own
harness showed it rather than abstract instruction: the plain design prompt
produced no authored tools, a single worked example induced spontaneous
authorship, and explicit instruction made attempts general across the group.
Tools that survived the gate$\to$self-test$\to$repair chain shipped in their
candidates' packages---e.g.\
\texttt{verify\_per\_row\_ordering} (Appendix~\ref{app:tool-example}), a
self-check the proposer invented after reading, in its experience digest,
that executor rollouts repeatedly failed to implement per-row adaptive
ordering.  The compiled siblings of one group thus differ in their
\emph{tool sets}, not merely their prompts---the property that separates
harness synthesis from prompt search~\citep{adas,latm}.  Rejected tools were dropped fail-closed on unfixable gate and self-test
failures, and the leakage controls are mechanical rather than aspirational:
a repair whose similarity to the original falls below the $0.55$ floor is
rejected as an attempted rewrite, and repair prompts containing evaluator or
target-score markers are refused outright (\S\ref{sec:method-safety}).

\paragraph{Observation 6: What remains hard.}
Spontaneous tool authorship is rare without a worked example or forcing---a
9B proposer adopts new capabilities from templates, not abstract
instructions---suggesting a small SFT phase on tool-authoring trajectories
as the next lever (our 30-step RL cap did not accommodate one).  Post-jump
plateaus remain unbroken by any prompt-level mechanism we tried; only new
information moved them.  And the results carry stated caveats: campaign
numbers are single-seed (winning programs were re-evaluated $5\times$ for
stability, but full campaigns were not re-run); one of our entries carries a provenance
caveat (LLM-SQL, Obs.~1), and in the transaction column the \emph{previous}
SOTA was produced under an evaluator that can accept invalid schedules, so
our validity-guarded $4{,}255$ is not directly comparable to it.

\subsection{Case Study: How a Harness Evolves}
\label{sec:exp-casestudy}

Because $M_0$ never changes, a task's whole trajectory can be read as a sequence
of harness edits.  Figure~\ref{fig:cp_evolution} plots every improving round of
the circle-packing campaign, and Table~\ref{tab:cp_components} lists what the
proposer added at each.  Three regimes are visible.  \emph{Generic search}
(r312--r622): the proposer's first generated tools are task-agnostic search
utilities---\texttt{run\_targeted\_search}, then \texttt{parameter\_expander}---
paired with reminder middlewares that enforce cheap probing before a scarce
evaluator call; these buy steady but small gains.  \emph{Geometric
specialization} (r760--r761): the tools become task-specific, first diagnostic
(\texttt{geometry\_validator} checks a candidate packing) and then constructive
(\texttt{spacing\_optimizer}), and the skill names change from
``search-strategy'' to ``hexagonal-packing-patterns''---the largest single jump
of the campaign ($2.302 \to 2.450$) follows immediately.  \emph{Bug-directed
repair} (r764--r765): the proposer ships \texttt{hexagonal\_construction}, a
generator for the whole lattice, and---after reading in its experience digest
that rollouts kept mis-indexing staggered rows---an \texttt{indexing\_reminder}
middleware whose text targets exactly that failure (\emph{``use cumulative
indexing, not \texttt{row\_idx*5+i}''}).

The progression is the paper's mechanism claim in miniature: the proposer moves
from generic scaffolding, to domain structure, to a named defect in the
executor's own behavior, and each step is a legible artifact rather than a
weight delta.  Declarative-only rounds (r620, r762, r763) show the complement:
some gains come purely from re-tuning iteration caps and temperature, which the
same policy also controls.

\subsection{Cross-Task Proposer Transfer}
\label{sec:exp-transfer}
A proposer is trained per task, yielding adapters $\phi_1,\dots,\phi_{11}$.  We
probe whether a proposer's learned harness-construction strategy transfers by
building the matrix of Figure~\ref{tab:transfer_matrix}: twelve source rows---the
untrained proposer $\phi_0$ plus the eleven task adapters---against all eleven
targets.  Entry $(i,j)$ is the percentage change in $\mathrm{Best@}6$ on task
$\tau_j$ when the harness is proposed by $\phi_i$, relative to $\phi_0$ on the
same task.  Every cell is zero-shot and starts from the same fixed initial
harness and seed program with no inheritance, no experience digest, and no
analyst pass, so it isolates what the adapter itself carries; cells are single
runs rather than repeated draws, which bounds how finely they can be read.  The diagonal measures
in-task proposer learning and the off-diagonal zero-shot cross-task transfer;
row means indicate which task yields the most general strategy, column means
which target is most helpable, and domain blocks whether mathematical,
algorithmic, and systems tasks cluster.  The result is negative and we report it as such: off-diagonal cells sit within a
few percent of the base proposer in both directions, and the row means span only
$-3.4\%$ to $+0.1\%$ once the ahc058 column---whose base score is near zero, so
ratios there explode---is set aside.  The in-task diagonal is no better: an
adapter applied to its own task, stripped of the campaign context it was trained
inside, does not beat the untrained proposer either.  A proposer trained on one task therefore
carries little that helps another task zero-shot, and the amortization claim of
\S\ref{sec:method-setup} should be read as amortization \emph{across rounds of a
task}, not across tasks.  This refines rather than overturns the main result, but
it does bound it.
