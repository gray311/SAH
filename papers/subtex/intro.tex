\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Large language models act in the world through \emph{agents}: a model embedded
in a harness---the software that supplies its system prompt, tools, skills,
context policy, and control loop~\citep{weng2026harness,anthropicagents,react,
toolformer,sweagent,autogen,voyager,webarena,osworld}.  How much of a model's
capability becomes measurable progress is decided by that harness, nowhere more
sharply than in \emph{discovery}, where a frozen LLM paired with a hand-built
evolutionary scaffold produces mathematical and algorithmic results beyond
strong baselines~\citep{funsearch,alphaevolve,alphaevolvemath,openevolve,
shinkaevolve,eoh,llmsr,aiscientist}.  A natural next step is
\emph{self-improvement}: letting an agent get better at a task from its own
experience, with no stronger model to learn from.  Today this takes one of two
forms (Figure~\ref{fig:taxonomy}a,b).  One updates the model's \emph{weights},
fine-tuning the executor on its own rollouts, as in test-time and reasoning
RL~\citep{tttdiscover,thetaevolve,ttrl,ttt,grpo,dapo,agenticrl,ragen}.  The
other keeps the model frozen and edits its \emph{harness}---prompts, tools,
context, or an external program archive---as an artifact revised by a
\emph{fixed} proposer~\citep{openevolve,funsearch,shinkaevolve,eoh,metaharness,
selfharness,hase,dgm,sica,godelagent,stop,alita,selfevolvesurvey}.

Both routes stop short of a reusable \emph{proposer}, in complementary ways.
Weight updates are \emph{inefficient}---each step costs many executor rollouts,
hundreds in reported test-time-RL configurations~\citep{tttdiscover,
thetaevolve}---and their benefit is \emph{not traceable}: an improvement
registers only as a diffuse change in the weights, never as a nameable tool,
skill, verification step, or search parameter, and it is simply unavailable when
the executor is frozen by policy, safety review, or an API boundary.  Harness
editing keeps its \emph{proposer} fixed: the same prompt-optimizer,
textual-gradient, or agent-design procedure~\citep{ape,opro,promptbreeder,
evoprompt,mipro,metaprompting,dspy,textgrad,trace,gepa,adas,aflow,agentsquare,
agentsymbolic} re-derives design knowledge from scratch on every run, and as
archives and contexts grow, history selection---what to keep, what to compress,
how to trade exploitation against diversity---becomes a second hard problem
solved anew each time, with nothing amortized into the proposer itself.  History
suggests a resolution: hand-designed pipelines gave way to searched ones, and
searched ones to \emph{learned} components~\citep{learn2learn,hypernetworks,
adas}.

We propose to learn the proposer itself (Figure~\ref{fig:taxonomy}c).  \method{}
keeps the executor $M_0$
\textbf{permanently frozen} and trains a harness \emph{proposer}
$\pi_\phi$---the same base model with a LoRA adapter~\citep{lora}---by
group-relative RL with a leave-one-out (RLOO) baseline~\citep{rloo,grpo,ragen}
to emit, per task, a complete harness specification: system prompt, skill
playbook, tool descriptions, sampling and control parameters, and, through a
generative channel, \emph{new executable tools} as Python
source~\citep{latm,creator,craft,voyager,trove}.  Each spec compiles
deterministically into a runnable agent package; the frozen executor rolls it
out under a hard evaluator budget; and a group's relative outcomes are the
proposer's only learning signal (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}).  Conditioned on the
incumbent best program and a digest of what every prior candidate did, scored,
and died of, the proposer internalizes a task's reward history into an amortized
search policy rather than a growing context---it learns to design, not merely to
sample.  Because single-solution rollouts make harness quality a high-variance
target, we re-evaluate top harnesses with multiple executor samples, so that
reported gains reflect the harness rather than a lucky draw
(\S\ref{sec:method}).

Three properties motivate this design.  \emph{Amortization}: search cost is paid
at training time, and synthesis for a new round is a single proposer episode,
the argument behind learned optimizers and
hypernetworks~\citep{learn2learn,hypernetworks}.  \emph{Expressiveness}: because
the generative channel carries executable code, the reachable harness space
exceeds prompt variation~\citep{adas,latm,voyager}---a proposed agent can differ
from its siblings in what machinery exists, not only in how it is described.
\emph{Attribution and safety}: freezing $M_0$ makes each improvement
attributable to an inspectable harness, but letting an optimizer write code that
runs inside an evaluation loop invites reward hacking and specification
gaming~\citep{skalse2022,specgaming,denison2024,baker2025,metr2025,
impossiblebench}.  We therefore make evaluation integrity a design invariant:
generated tools act only through a capability-bounded SDK that cannot reach the
evaluator, pass fail-closed static gates and sandboxed self-tests, and are
repaired---when broken---by \emph{the same frozen model} under a localization
guard, so the repair channel can fix bugs but cannot inject knowledge
(\S\ref{sec:method-safety}).

We evaluate on eleven hard problems spanning AlphaEvolve-style mathematical
construction~\citep{alphaevolve,alphaevolvemath}---the Erd\H{o}s minimum-overlap
problem, two autocorrelation inequalities, circle packing, and maximal Hadamard
determinants---four systems-algorithm benchmarks from the ADRS
suite~\citep{barbarians}, and two AtCoder Heuristic Contest
problems~\citep{ahc,alebench}, all drawn from the evaluation suite whose
discovery-finetuned Finch models we take as the matched-scale
baseline~\citep{finch}.  Under a pre-registered budget of at most 30 RL steps,
the learned proposer's harnesses carry a frozen 9B executor~\citep{qwen3} past
Finch-9B on \textbf{all eleven} tasks and match or exceed the published
$\le$10B state of the art on \textbf{seven}: they set the best score on the
Erd\H{o}s minimum-overlap problem, ahc039, ahc058, EPLB, PRISM, and LLM-SQL, and
tie it on circle packing---the two mathematical constructions where the previous
best came from test-time-RL and evolutionary methods that adapt the executor
directly~\citep{tttdiscover,thetaevolve,qu2026coral,liu2026evox}---whereas the
same executor under the fixed initial harness beats Finch-9B on only three
(Table~\ref{tab:main_results}).  On Erd\H{o}s the frozen executor also passes the
best known human construction ($0.380919$ vs.\ $0.380927$), having written its
own continuous optimizer under a harness that never supplied one.  A breakthrough ledger (Table~\ref{tab:ledger})
traces each jump to sampling breadth striking a new program family, the
inheritance ratchet compounding it, or new information entering the system; the
two hardest tasks fell not to more iteration but to \emph{diagnosis}---an
evaluation timeout that structurally favored no-op programs, and a scoring rule
whose structure the executor had never been told.  We take this as the paper's
central finding: much of what reads as a small model's capability ceiling is a
harness that has not told the executor what game it is playing---and a trained
proposer, fed the right experience, learns to close exactly that gap.
