\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Large language models act in the world through \emph{agents}: a model
embedded in a harness, the surrounding software that defines its system
prompt, tools, skills, context policy, and control
loop~\citep{weng2026harness,anthropicagents}.  Tool-using agents interleave
reasoning with external actions~\citep{react,toolformer}; coding agents
succeed or fail with the design of their agent--computer
interface~\citep{sweagent,autogen}; embodied and web agents live inside
hand-built perception--action scaffolds~\citep{voyager,webarena,osworld};
and verbal-feedback loops turn a fixed model into an iterative
improver~\citep{reflexion,selfrefine}.  The lesson is sharpest in
\emph{discovery}, where an agent iteratively edits a program against a
black-box evaluator: FunSearch and AlphaEvolve pair a frozen LLM with a
carefully engineered evolutionary scaffold and produce mathematical and
algorithmic results beyond human baselines~\citep{funsearch,alphaevolve,
alphaevolvemath,openevolve,shinkaevolve,eoh,llmsr,aiscientist}---the weights
supply raw capability, while the harness decides how much of it becomes
measurable progress.  Agent behavior is thus a property of the coupled
model--harness system, not of the weights alone; yet the harnesses behind
these results are hand-built per domain by experts, through trial and error
that never accumulates into a reusable artifact.

At its core, hard-problem discovery is the problem of \emph{transporting
previous rewards to future attempts}: the agent must let the scores of
earlier solutions reshape where it searches next.  Existing methods differ
mainly in \emph{what object} the reward updates, and hence in how it reaches
the next solution.  \textbf{Executor-weight adaptation} folds reward directly
into the solution-generating model: test-time RL fine-tunes the solver on its
own rollouts, so $(\tau,r)\!\to\!\Delta\theta\!\to\!M_{\theta'}\!\to\!y$
\citep{tttdiscover,thetaevolve,ttrl,ttt}.  \textbf{Context and artifact
evolution} keeps the model frozen and instead updates an external object---a
program archive, a running context, or a self-edited harness---that is fed
back in, so $(\tau,r)\!\to\!H'\!\to\!M_0(\cdot\mid H')\!\to\!y$
\citep{openevolve,funsearch,shinkaevolve,metaharness,selfharness,hase}.  Both
routes work, and both stop short of a reusable \emph{designer}: the first
retrains the solver for every problem, and the second, as archives and
contexts grow, turns history selection---what to keep, what to compress, how
to trade exploitation against diversity---into a second hard problem solved
anew each run by a \emph{fixed} proposer.

We take a third route.  We keep the executor frozen but train the object that
maps reward to behavior: an independent harness \emph{proposer}
$\pi_\phi$, so that
$(\tau,r)\!\to\!\Delta\phi\!\to\!H\!\sim\!\pi_{\phi'}\!\to\!M_0(\cdot\mid
H)\!\to\!y$.  Rather than editing one harness with a fixed proposer, we
\emph{update the policy that proposes harnesses}, internalizing a task's
reward history into the proposer's weights so that it forms an amortized
search policy---under a fixed-size input it drifts toward high-return harness
constructions instead of re-deriving them from a growing context.  Two
consequences follow.  First, adaptation and execution are cleanly separated:
$M_0$ supplies capability and $\pi_\phi$ supplies the search strategy, so
every improvement is carried by an explicit, executable, inspectable
harness---a bottleneck that gives artifact-level attribution that direct
weight updates lack.  Second, because the proposer, not the solver, absorbs
the gradient, each optimization step spends only $K\!=\!8$ executor
rollouts---one solution per candidate harness---against the $512$-solution
steps typical of executor-weight methods~\citep{tttdiscover,thetaevolve}, a
$64\times$ reduction \emph{per step}; we therefore compare score-versus-
rollout frontiers rather than claiming a fixed total-compute ratio, since
total cost also depends on step count, proposer forward/backward passes, and
harness runtime.

We introduce \method{} to realize this route.  The executor $M_0$ is
\textbf{permanently frozen}; the proposer $\pi_\phi$---the same base model
with a LoRA adapter~\citep{lora}---is trained with group-relative
reinforcement learning~\citep{grpo,rloo} to emit, per task, a complete
harness specification: system prompt, skill playbook, tool descriptions,
sampling and control parameters, and, through a generative channel,
\emph{new executable tools} as Python source.  Each spec compiles
deterministically into a runnable agent package; the frozen executor rolls it
out under a hard evaluator budget; and a group's relative outcomes are the
proposer's only learning signal (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}).  The proposer is
conditioned on \emph{experience}---the incumbent best program and harness and
a digest of what every previous candidate did, scored, and died of---so it
learns to design, not merely to sample.  A key assumption is that rollout
score under the frozen executor is a faithful measure of harness quality; we
discuss where this frays---evaluator noise, timeout artifacts, and the
single-solution variance that can mistake a lucky rollout for a good
harness---and how we guard against it by re-evaluating top harnesses with
multiple executor samples (\S\ref{sec:method}).

Three properties motivate this formulation.  \emph{Amortization}: search cost
is paid at training time, and synthesis for a new round is a single proposer
episode, the argument that motivates learned optimizers and
hypernetworks~\citep{learn2learn,hypernetworks}.  \emph{Expressiveness}:
because the spec's generative channel carries executable code, the reachable
harness space is not limited to prompt variations~\citep{adas,latm,voyager}---a
proposed agent can differ from its siblings in what machinery exists, not
only in how it is described.  \emph{Attribution and safety}: freezing $M_0$
makes every point of improvement attributable to the harness and keeps the
executor's behavioral properties intact---but letting an optimizer write code
that runs inside an evaluation loop invites reward hacking and specification
gaming~\citep{skalse2022,specgaming,denison2024,baker2025,metr2025,
impossiblebench}.  We therefore make evaluation integrity a design invariant:
generated tools act only through a capability-bounded SDK that cannot reach
the evaluator, pass fail-closed static gates and sandboxed self-tests, and are
repaired---when broken---by \emph{the same frozen model} under a localization
guard, so the repair channel can fix bugs but cannot inject knowledge
(\S\ref{sec:method-safety}).

We evaluate on eleven discovery tasks spanning the mathematical constructions
popularized by the AlphaEvolve line~\citep{alphaevolve,alphaevolvemath}---the
Erd\H{o}s minimum-overlap problem, two autocorrelation inequalities, circle
packing, and maximal Hadamard determinants---four systems-algorithm
benchmarks from the ADRS suite~\citep{barbarians}, and two AtCoder Heuristic
Contest problems~\citep{ahc,alebench}, all drawn from the evaluation suite of
the discovery-finetuning work whose Finch models we take as our matched-scale
baseline~\citep{finch}.  Under a pre-registered budget of at most 30 RL steps,
the learned proposer's harnesses carry the frozen 9B
executor~\citep{qwen3} past the discovery-finetuned Finch-9B~\citep{finch} on
\textbf{all eleven tasks}, and match or exceed the published $\le$10B state of
the art on \textbf{seven}: they set the best score on ahc039, ahc058, EPLB,
PRISM, and LLM-SQL, and match it on the Erd\H{o}s minimum-overlap problem and
circle packing---the two hardest constructions, where the previous best comes
from test-time-RL and evolutionary methods~\citep{tttdiscover,thetaevolve}
that adapt the solver directly.  The same executor under the fixed initial
harness beats Finch-9B on only three tasks (Table~\ref{tab:main_results}).  The proposer also exercises the
generative channel end to end, authoring novel tools that survive review and
change the compiled agent's tool set (\S\ref{sec:exp-results}).  Consistent
with our scoping, these gains are obtained with one task-specific proposer per
task; we treat cross-task and cross-model transfer as open questions and
study them directly (\S\ref{sec:exp-transfer}) rather than assuming them.

Beyond the headline comparison, the campaign is instrumented so that every
score movement is explainable.  A breakthrough ledger
(Table~\ref{tab:ledger}) traces each jump to sampling breadth striking a new
program family, to the inheritance ratchet compounding it, or to new
information entering the system; the two hardest tasks fell not to more
iteration but to \emph{diagnosis}---an evaluation timeout that structurally
favored no-op programs, and a scoring rule whose structure the executor had
never been told.  We take this as the paper's central qualitative finding:
much of what reads as a small model's capability ceiling is a harness that
has not told the executor what game it is playing---and a trained proposer,
fed the right experience, learns to close exactly that gap.
