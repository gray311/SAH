\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Self-evolution by adapting the model.}  One family improves an agent
by changing the model itself.  Test-time training and test-time RL adapt weights
per instance or per problem~\citep{ttt,ttrl,s1}; for discovery specifically,
TTT-Discover~\citep{tttdiscover} and ThetaEvolve~\citep{thetaevolve} fine-tune
the executor on its own evolutionary rollouts and hold the strongest published
$\le$10B results on several of our tasks, at hundreds of executor rollouts per
step.  Discovery-finetuning instead amortizes the skill \emph{across} tasks by
mid-training on large evolution-trajectory corpora, as in the EFT/Finch models
we adopt as a matched-scale baseline~\citep{finch}.  A parallel line lets an
agent rewrite its own code or scaffold---STOP~\citep{stop}, the Darwin G\"odel
Machine~\citep{dgm}, self-improving coding agents~\citep{sica,godelagent}, and
minimal-predefinition generalists~\citep{alita}, with surveys mapping the
space~\citep{selfevolvesurvey,sia}.  All of these couple the improver and the
improved, so gains are diffuse and hard to attribute and are unavailable when
the executor is frozen by policy or an API boundary.  \method{} instead keeps
the executor permanently frozen and learns only a separate proposer---matching
or exceeding these weight-adapting methods, including on the Erd\H{o}s and
circle-packing constructions where they held the prior best, at a far smaller
per-step rollout budget.

\paragraph{Self-evolution by editing the harness.}  A second family keeps the
model frozen and instead revises an external artifact---a program archive,
prompts, a context, or a self-edited scaffold.  Evolutionary loops around frozen
LLMs produce state-of-the-art discovery programs: FunSearch~\citep{funsearch},
AlphaEvolve and its mathematical campaigns~\citep{alphaevolve,alphaevolvemath},
sample-efficient successors~\citep{openevolve,shinkaevolve}, heuristic
evolution~\citep{eoh}, equation discovery~\citep{llmsr}, reward
design~\citep{eureka}, research agents~\citep{aiscientist}, and concurrent
multi-agent and meta-evolution systems~\citep{qu2026coral,liu2026evox}, over
benchmarks for algorithm engineering~\citep{alebench} and systems
research~\citep{barbarians}.  A related body optimizes the text or structure
around the model: instruction and prompt
search~\citep{ape,opro,promptbreeder,evoprompt,metaprompting}, prompt
compilation~\citep{dspy,mipro}, textual gradients~\citep{textgrad,trace},
reflective evolution~\citep{gepa}, automated agent and workflow
design~\citep{adas,aflow,agentsquare,agentsymbolic}, and meta/self-editing
harnesses~\citep{metaharness,selfharness,hase,voyager,reflexion,selfrefine}.
Across all of these the harness is revised by a \emph{fixed, unlearned}
proposer: design knowledge is re-derived per task at deployment time and never
amortized, and as archives and contexts grow, history selection becomes a second
hard problem solved anew each run.  We make the proposer the trained object, so
design knowledge accrues in its weights across tasks and rounds, and its action
space includes executable tool code admitted through a leakage-controlled review
chain.
