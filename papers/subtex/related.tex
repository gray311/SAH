\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Self-evolution by adapting the model.}  One family improves an agent
by changing model weights.  Test-time training and test-time RL adapt weights
per instance or per problem~\citep{ttt,ttrl,s1}; for discovery specifically,
TTT-Discover~\citep{tttdiscover} and ThetaEvolve~\citep{thetaevolve} fine-tune
the executor on its own search rollouts and held the strongest published
$\le$10B results on five of our tasks, at hundreds of executor rollouts per
training step.  Rather than adapt per problem, discovery fine-tuning amortizes
the skill \emph{across} tasks by training on large evolution-trajectory corpora,
as in the EFT/Finch models we adopt as a matched-scale baseline~\citep{finch}.
Closest to us are systems that co-evolve weights \emph{and} harness together:
HASE~\citep{hase} and SIA~\citep{sia} update both, coupling the improver to the
improved.  Because the executor's weights move, gains in this family are diffuse
and hard to attribute to any nameable tool, skill, or procedure, and the family
is inapplicable whenever the executor is frozen by policy or served behind an
API.  \method{} keeps the executor untouched and trains only a separate proposer
policy---a LoRA on a copy of the same base model---so no gradient reaches the
executor; on the Erd\H{o}s construction, where TTT-Discover held the prior $\le$10B
best, a frozen executor under a learned harness now exceeds it, and it
reaches $95\%$ of ThetaEvolve's circle-packing record.

\paragraph{Self-evolution by editing the harness.}  A second family keeps the
model frozen and revises an external artifact---a program archive, prompts, a
context, or the agent's own scaffold.  Evolutionary program search around frozen
LLMs produces state-of-the-art discovery programs: FunSearch~\citep{funsearch},
AlphaEvolve and its mathematical campaigns~\citep{alphaevolve,alphaevolvemath},
open reimplementations and sample-efficient
successors~\citep{openevolve,shinkaevolve}, heuristic evolution~\citep{eoh},
equation discovery~\citep{llmsr}, reward design~\citep{eureka}, and concurrent
multi-agent and meta-evolution systems~\citep{qu2026coral,liu2026evox},
evaluated on suites for algorithm engineering~\citep{alebench} and on the
systems-research problems of~\citet{barbarians}; end-to-end research
agents~\citep{aiscientist} automate the surrounding pipeline.  A parallel line
lets a frozen model rewrite its own code or scaffold: STOP~\citep{stop}, the
Darwin G\"odel Machine~\citep{dgm}, self-improving coding
agents~\citep{sica,godelagent}, and generalists that synthesize their own
capabilities~\citep{alita}, with a survey mapping the
space~\citep{selfevolvesurvey}.  A third optimizes the text or structure around
the model: instruction and prompt
search~\citep{ape,opro,promptbreeder,evoprompt,metaprompting}, prompt
compilation~\citep{dspy,mipro}, textual gradients~\citep{textgrad,trace},
reflective evolution~\citep{gepa}, automated agent and workflow
design~\citep{adas,aflow,agentsquare,agentsymbolic}, and harnesses optimized or
edited as a whole~\citep{metaharness,selfharness}; relatedly, in-context
self-correction improves outputs without touching the harness at
all~\citep{voyager,reflexion,selfrefine}.  Across this family the proposer's
\emph{weights are fixed}: design knowledge is re-derived per task at deployment
time rather than accumulated in a policy that gets better at proposing.  We make
the proposer the trained object, so design knowledge accrues in its weights
across tasks and rounds.  Its action space also includes executable tool code;
unlike tool-creation methods that abstract functions from solution
traces~\citep{latm,creator,craft,trove}, the tools here are emitted by a
\emph{learned} policy as part of a harness and admitted through a
leakage-controlled review chain (\S\ref{sec:method-safety}).
